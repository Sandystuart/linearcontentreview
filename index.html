Common Questions

What people ask before filing

What kinds of disputes can you actually rule on?

Anything with a documented paper trail to check: disputed famous ancestors, two branches of one surname with no proven link, unexplained DNA matches, or two family trees that can't both be right. If there's no surviving record either way, I'll tell you that directly rather than take the case.

What if the record doesn't clearly support either side?

You still get a findings report. It states plainly what the record can and can't confirm — that's a real answer, even when it's not a clean yes or no.

How long does a case take?

Turnaround depends on how far back the disputed claim goes and how available the records are. Recent, well-documented lines move faster than claims reaching back centuries.

Is my family's information kept private?

Yes. Everything you share — names, dates, documents, DNA results — is used only to research your case and isn't shared with the other party or anyone else beyond what's needed for the findings report.

What do I need to have ready before filing?

Names, approximate dates, locations, and anything you already have — an existing family tree, DNA test results, old letters, family lore. Fragments are fine; I'll follow up if I need more.

Is the $495 fee refundable if the case can't be resolved?

The fee covers the full research effort and a written findings report either way — including cases where the record's answer is inconclusive. That inconclusive finding is still a deliverable, not a refund situation.
